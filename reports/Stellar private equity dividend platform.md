# Gate shares at the protocol, pay dividends in USDC

The strongest Stellar design for this platform issues each company's share token as a **classic Stellar asset with `AUTH_REQUIRED | AUTH_REVOCABLE | AUTH_CLAWBACK_ENABLED`**, exposed to Soroban through its Stellar Asset Contract (SAC). A Soroban gate contract verifies a **Groth16/BN254 proof of non-US residence**, records a **Poseidon nullifier**, and then authorizes the investor's trustline. Because the protocol refuses transfers to any unauthorized account, the bonding curve, the native AMM, Soroswap, path payments and the lock vault all stay compliance-gated with no transfer hooks. That largely resolves the apparent conflict between Reg S gating and open AMM trading, at the price of a one-time proof before anyone can buy. On the money side, **Circle Mint** (KYB, USD wire to native Stellar USDC) is the credible production rail for companies depositing profits. **MoneyGram Ramps** (SEP-24, cash-out in 170+ countries) plus a regional bank anchor such as Bitso, BlindPay or Anclap covers investor cash-out. The demo should use **testanchor.stellar.org with Circle's testnet USDC** (`GBBD47…FLA5`). The design rests on two facts. First, Reg S defines a US person by **residence, not nationality**, so off-the-shelf passport-nationality proofs aren't enough. Second, pump.fun's curve multiplies the price about **14.7×** before graduation, which, if the curve isn't flattened, prices a cash-yielding token far away from its fundamentals. The simulation should test that second point head-on.

## Five primitives carry the "Stellar Unlocked" story

The hackathon track highlights core-protocol features, and this product can use most of them for real rather than as decoration. BN254 (CAP-74) and Poseidon/Poseidon2 (CAP-75) went live on mainnet with **Protocol 25 "X-Ray" on 2026-01-22**. Protocol 26 (2026-05-06) added CAP-80, which brings BN254 multi-scalar multiplication (MSM) and scalar-field (Fr) arithmetic, and CAP-73, which lets a SAC create a G-account trustline mid-invocation ([Stellar software versions](https://developers.stellar.org/docs/networks/software-versions); [CAP-0080](https://github.com/stellar/stellar-protocol/blob/master/core/cap-0080.md)). SDF ships a `groth16_verifier` example with BN254 and BLS12-381 contracts that accept snarkjs-format fixtures. It is marked demo-only and unaudited, and its BN254 path has been tested only with Gnark fixtures, so snarkjs G2 coordinate ordering should be tested on day one ([soroban-examples](https://github.com/stellar/soroban-examples/tree/main/groth16_verifier)). Poseidon on-chain is a permutation, not a hash. Use [stellar/rs-soroban-poseidon](https://github.com/stellar/rs-soroban-poseidon) so the parameters match circomlib ([Stellar ZK docs](https://developers.stellar.org/docs/build/apps/zk)).

The rest of the toolkit is classic. Issuer flags plus `SetTrustLineFlags` give a protocol-enforced allowlist, and the SAC applies the same flags to contract (C-address) balances, so a curve or vault contract must itself be authorized ([Control asset access](https://developers.stellar.org/docs/tokens/control-asset-access); [SAC docs](https://developers.stellar.org/docs/tokens/stellar-asset-contract)). The native constant-product AMM charges a **fixed 0.30%** and requires fully authorized trustlines on both assets. Revoking an LP's authorization force-withdraws it into claimable balances, and `PathPaymentStrictSend/StrictReceive` route across both the order book (SDEX) and the native pools ([Liquidity on Stellar](https://developers.stellar.org/docs/learn/fundamentals/liquidity-on-stellar-sdex-liquidity-pools)). Claimable balances (CAP-23) support time predicates but allow at most 10 claimants and lock 0.5 XLM per claimant ([CAP-23](https://github.com/stellar/stellar-protocol/blob/master/core/cap-0023.md)). Clawback (CAP-35) burns a holder's balance without their signature, and the flag is visible on the public ledger ([CAP-35](https://github.com/stellar/stellar-protocol/blob/master/core/cap-0035.md)).

There is a supply-locking subtlety. Stellar's docs fix supply by setting the issuer's master weight to 0, and note that the SAC admin must then still be the issuer ([Control asset access](https://developers.stellar.org/docs/tokens/control-asset-access)). A locked issuer, though, can never authorize another investor's trustline. The workable pattern is to mint the full supply to the curve, then `set_admin` the SAC to a gate/admin contract whose code exposes `set_authorized` (proof-gated) and timelocked `clawback` but no `mint`. The supply guarantee then moves from "dead keys" to "audited code with upgrades disabled or timelocked". This inference should be checked on testnet before it goes into the architecture doc: confirm that SAC admin calls succeed from a non-issuer admin.

## Residence-based ZK gating resolves most of the AMM tension

Reg S §230.902(k)(1) defines a US person as "any natural person resident in the United States", plus US-organized entities, trusts with US trustees and similar ([17 CFR 230.902](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/17/230.902)). A nationality proof gets it wrong in both directions. A US citizen living in Lisbon is generally not a Reg S US person, while a French national living in New York is. zkPassport and Self prove passport attributes such as nationality, and both target EVM. Neither has a Stellar integration ([zkPassport quick start](https://docs.zkpassport.id/getting-started/quick-start); [Self docs](https://docs.self.xyz/)). The circuit should therefore assert `residence_country ≠ USA`, `entity_incorporation_country ≠ USA` for corporate buyers, credential expiry > now, and optionally `kyc_level ≥ X`. Its public outputs should be `nullifier = Poseidon(secret, offering_id)` and the investor's Stellar address, so a proof can't be replayed or front-run. SEP-9 already carries the right input: `address_country_code` is residence, and there is no nationality field at all ([SEP-9](https://github.com/stellar/stellar-protocol/blob/master/ecosystem/sep-0009.md)). Reg S also requires the buyer to be **outside the US when the order is placed**. A credential can't prove location at each trade, so pair it with a per-session geofence or self-attestation in the UI. Avoid "directed selling efforts" too: marketing in channels with general US circulation can condition the US market even if every buyer is offshore.

Cost is not a constraint. One community tool claims about 12M CPU instructions per Groth16 verify (**unverified, single source**) ([stellar-zk](https://awesome.ecosyste.ms/projects/github.com%2Fsalazarsebas%2Fstellar-zk)). Nethermind measured optimized Noir UltraHonk verification at **about 80M of the 400M per-transaction instruction cap**, with a live testnet verify costing 0.01242 XLM ([Nethermind](https://www.nethermind.io/blog/making-noir-verification-cheaper-on-stellar)). Groth16 on BN254 is the cheapest and simplest hackathon route. UltraHonk avoids a per-circuit trusted setup and is the route to verifying zkPassport-style Noir circuits later.

### The trading tension and five ways to resolve it

The tension is real for any token with transfer hooks. In an ERC-3643 design, every pool contract has to be a registered identity, which conflicts with permissionless AMMs ([OZ RWA docs](https://docs.openzeppelin.com/stellar-contracts/tokens/rwa/rwa)). SEP-8 approval servers need a co-signature on every transfer, and they effectively can't pool, because trustlines sit at maintain-liabilities ([SEP-8](https://github.com/stellar/stellar-protocol/blob/master/ecosystem/sep-0008.md)). The classic `AUTH_REQUIRED` model largely avoids this. Pool-share trustlines can't even be created without authorized underlying trustlines, and SAC transfers to unauthorized G- or C-addresses fail ([Liquidity on Stellar](https://developers.stellar.org/docs/learn/fundamentals/liquidity-on-stellar-sdex-liquidity-pools); [SAC](https://developers.stellar.org/docs/tokens/stellar-asset-contract)). The AMM is then open among the allowlist, the way a members-only exchange is open to its members.

| Option | How gating works | Trading UX | Regulatory fit | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Classic AUTH_REQUIRED + ZK gate → `set_authorized`** | Protocol enforces it on every venue | One-time proof, then native AMM, SDEX, path payments and Soroswap (if the pair is authorized) | Strong. Holders can be revoked if they move to the US | **Recommended** |
| B. KYC on primary only, open secondary (Centrifuge deRWA pattern) | Hooks on vault subscribe/redeem only | Fully permissionless secondary | Weak for Reg S resale restrictions during the distribution compliance period ([Centrifuge docs](https://docs.centrifuge.io/developer/protocol/features/token-compliance/)) | Not for launch |
| C. OZ RWA (ERC-3643) custom SEP-41 token with `country_restrict`, `initial_lockup_period`, `max_balance` | Contract hooks plus identity registry | Soroban venues only; loses native SDEX/AMM/path payments | Rich per-transfer rules | Use if per-transfer rules (holder caps, lockups) become mandatory |
| D. SEP-8 approval server | Issuer co-signs each transaction | Breaks pools and composability | Strong | Avoid |
| E. Time-phased: Option A during the compliance period, relaxed afterwards | Flag policy changes over time | Improves with time | Needs counsel; Category 3 resale limits apply to US-issuer equity | Production roadmap item |

Option A has three costs, and the pitch should name them. Discovery stops being permissionless: a memecoin tourist can't buy on impulse. Each holder needs a 0.5 XLM trustline reserve, which the platform can sponsor. And the issuer's clawback power is visible on the ledger, which the platform should present as investor protection (court orders, lost keys) and fence with a governance timelock. Precedent supports the pattern. Stellar's tokenized RWAs reached about **$3.996B on 2026-08-29** (Stellar Dune dashboard via press), and the dominant compliance model there is issuer allowlisting plus clawback, with off-chain transfer-agent books, as in Franklin's BENJI ([Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/stellar-tokenized-rwa-market-nears-4b-after-fourfold-2026-growth); [eco.com, secondary](https://eco.com/support/en/articles/15254016-benji-deep-dive-2026-franklin-templeton-s-tokenized-money-market)). The notes found **no live tokenized private equity on Stellar**. DTCC's tokenized Russell 1000 equities on Stellar are targeted for H1 2027 ([Ledger Insights](https://www.ledgerinsights.com/dtc-tokenization-service-to-add-stellar-as-second-public-blockchain/)). The platform would be early, and that makes the legal structure more important than the code.

## Circle Mint in, MoneyGram out, testanchor for the demo

The company and the investor need different rails, and a pitch that uses one consumer anchor for both will sound naive. For the company depositing profits, **Circle Mint** is the production answer: a KYB-gated business account, USD wire in, native USDC on Stellar out, and wire back to fiat ([Circle: USDC on Stellar](https://www.circle.com/multi-chain-usdc/stellar)). Mainnet USDC is `GA5ZSEJYB37JRC5AVCIA5MOP4RHTM335X2KGX3IHOJAPP5RE34K4KZVN` ([Circle addresses](https://developers.circle.com/stablecoins/usdc-contract-addresses)). Companies that already hold USDC on another chain can bridge it through **CCTP (Stellar domain 27)**. Stellar is a destination for all CCTP transfers but supports only Standard Transfer as a source ([Circle CCTP](https://developers.circle.com/cctp/cctp-supported-blockchains)). For investors cashing out, **MoneyGram Ramps** runs on SEP-10 plus SEP-24, with cash-out in 170+ countries per secondary coverage. It is a retail cash network, with cash-in in only about 25 countries, so it is the wrong tool for corporate profit deposits ([MoneyGram Developer Portal](https://developer.moneygram.com/moneygram-developer/docs/integrate-moneygram-ramps); [CryptoGames](https://cryptogames.gg/moneygram-ramps-goes-live-on-solana-ending-stellar-exclusivity/)). Its US exclusions (Alaska, Hawaii, Louisiana, New York) don't matter for a non-US investor base. Bank payouts come from regional USDC anchors in the SDF directory: **BlindPay** (Argentina, Brazil, Colombia, Mexico, US), **Bitso**, **Anclap** and **ChipperCash** ([Anchor Directory](https://anchors.stellar.org/); [BlindPay docs](https://blindpay.com/docs/stablecoin/)). Third-party monitoring flags **Mykobo** (delisted 2026-09-06), **Bitnovo** (HTTP 502), **Clpx** (withdrawals disabled) and **Cowrie** (NGNT, not USDC) as ones to avoid. That monitoring isn't authoritative ([stellar-intel #1427](https://github.com/ezedike-evan/stellar-intel/issues/1427)). No fees or limits were confirmed for any provider.

For the testnet demo, **testanchor.stellar.org** implements SEP-6, 10, 12, 24, 31 and 38. It lists **Circle's testnet USDC issuer `GBBD47IF6LWK7P7MDEVSCWR7DPUWV3NY3DTQEVFL4NAT4AQH3ZLLFLA5`**, so real testnet USDC round-trips through it ([testanchor stellar.toml](https://testanchor.stellar.org/.well-known/stellar.toml)). With `@stellar/typescript-wallet-sdk`, the flow is `anchor.sep24().deposit/withdraw`, which returns an iframe URL, with a watcher that builds the withdrawal payment at `pending_user_transfer_start` ([Wallet SDK SEP-24](https://developers.stellar.org/docs/build/apps/wallet/sep24)). Fund accounts from Friendbot, add the USDC trustline, and top up from faucet.circle.com (Stellar option) ([Circle quickstart](https://developers.circle.com/stablecoins/quickstart-transfer-usdc-stellar)). Show the company deposit as a SEP-24 deposit (or label a faucet top-up "Circle Mint, simulated"), run `distribute()` on-chain, and show the investor withdrawing through the testanchor iframe. MoneyGram's sandbox needs Partner Portal approval, so don't put it on the critical path. The **Stellar Disbursement Platform** with a Circle distribution account is the production story for paying investors who have no wallet. It identifies receivers by phone or email and doesn't compute pro-rata amounts, and it is too heavy for the hackathon ([SDP Circle config](https://developers.stellar.org/docs/platforms/stellar-disbursement-platform/admin-guide/user-interface/circle-configuration); [SDP getting started](https://developers.stellar.org/docs/platforms/stellar-disbursement-platform/admin-guide/getting-started)). One open check: the notes did not verify whether Circle's USDC issuer is itself revocable or clawback-enabled, so confirm it on stellar.expert before claiming dividends can't be frozen.

## A four-contract MVP, with versions pinned deliberately

The recommended architecture has four Soroban contracts and one classic keeper. The **registry** stores company profiles, KYB attestation hashes, the agreement hash, the payout percentage and schedule, and social/financial links. The **eligibility gate** holds the Groth16 BN254 verifier, the Poseidon Merkle root of issued credentials and the nullifier set, and is SAC admin for each share asset. The **launchpad/curve**, forked from Hooks, uses a USDC reserve instead of Etherfuse stablebonds. The **lock vault** holds the epoch-keyed reward-per-share accumulator. A backend **graduation keeper** G-account receives curve proceeds and submits the classic `LiquidityPoolDeposit`, then locks itself with master weight 0. Hooks already shows the anti-griefing step that matters at graduation: if someone pre-seeded the pool at a bad price, swap it back to the curve price before depositing ([Hooks](https://github.com/adrianvrj/hooks)). Mercury or another listed indexer feeds trade history, because Horizon "will be deprecated" ([Stellar indexers](https://developers.stellar.org/docs/data/indexers)). Investors onboard with **smart-account-kit** passkeys, with gas sponsored through **OZ Channels** behind a Next.js route handler, and Freighter via Stellar Wallets Kit for crypto-native users ([smart-account-kit](https://github.com/kalepail/smart-account-kit); [OZ Channels](https://docs.openzeppelin.com/relayer/guides/stellar-channels-guide)). One catch: exchanges don't support withdrawals from contract accounts. Dividends to C-addresses work, but CEX cash-out needs a G-account or an anchor ([Smart wallets](https://developers.stellar.org/docs/build/apps/smart-wallets)).

```
Company (SEP-10) ─► KYB ladder ─► Registry(agreement hash, X%, schedule) ─► create asset + SAC ─► Curve(all supply)
Investor (passkey) ─► KYC issuer signs credential ─► browser snarkjs proof ─► Gate(verify BN254, Poseidon nullifier) ─► SAC.set_authorized
Investor ─USDC─► Curve.buy ─► (threshold) ─► Keeper G-acct ─► native LP deposit ─► SDEX/AMM/path payments
Holder ─► LockVault.lock(epoch) ◄─ Company.distribute(USDC via SEP-24 deposit / Circle Mint) ─► claim ─► SEP-24 withdraw
```

A vault design detail decides whether the dividend model works. Only tokens locked for the **whole epoch** should earn, or traders will lock just before `distribute()` and dump just after. Key positions by `(holder, epoch)`, or snapshot `total_locked` at epoch start. Extend position TTLs on every interaction, and never keep per-user data in temporary storage. Protocol 23 auto-restores archived persistent entries, but the restore costs a fee ([State archival](https://developers.stellar.org/docs/learn/fundamentals/contract-development/storage/state-archival)). A second decision is commercial, not technical: **where the curve's USDC goes**. In pure pump.fun style it all seeds the LP, and the company earns only a creator fee (Hooks uses 0.5%), which is a weak reason for a real business to hand over X% of its profits. If part of the curve's proceeds go to the company, the launch becomes a primary securities offering. Either way, the answer should be explicit in the README.

## Simulate the curve against cash flows, not memes against hope

The data set up a sharp contrast. On pump.fun, **fewer than 2% of launches graduate**. Estimates run from 0.63% (Sept 2025) to 1.89% (lifetime to May 2026), and 2.55% among tokens with 30 or more swaps ([arXiv 2602.14860](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.14860v1); [CoinPaprika](https://coinpaprika.com/education/token-graduation-mean-numbers/)). After graduating, the median token falls about 50% within ten minutes (a developer blog, not peer-reviewed) ([launchtape](https://dev.to/launchtape/what-2193-pumpfun-graduations-did-in-their-first-10-minutes-and-what-it-means-for-your-bot-4f70)). Among wallets that traded more than 10 tokens, about 60% lost money, and only 311 of 4.26M made over $1M ([BeInCrypto](https://beincrypto.com/pump-fun-trading-data-majority-lose-money/)). On the other side, small US businesses sell for **2.6–2.7× seller's discretionary earnings (SDE)** ([BizBuySell](https://www.bizbuysell.com/news/media_insight.html)). BLS data shows **51.5% of establishments surviving five years** ([BLS](https://www.bls.gov/bdm/us_age_naics_00_table7.txt)). The yield comparators are Main Street Capital at roughly 6–7.8% ([StockAnalysis](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/main/dividend/)) and RealT properties with nominal yields of 3–14%, whose secondary token prices often sit 50–85% below issue (unofficial tracker) ([RealT tracker](https://ehpst.duckdns.org/realt_rent_tracker/portfolio/17655)). Only 6.4% of security token offerings (STOs) ever traded freely ([Lambert et al.](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11187-021-00539-9)).

Several figures must stay out. **Do not use** the 0.198% graduation rate, which comes from withdrawn preprint arXiv 2607.02823 ([Brave New Coin](https://bravenewcoin.com/insights/what-auditing-832941-pump-fun-launches-taught-me-about-memecoin-success)). Do not use any sniper-cohort number from arXiv 2607.02795, whose authors withdrew all numerical results after finding about 43% of "buy" rows were sells ([correction](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.02795)). Treat as unverified: "97% of traders lose", the "96% lose" figure (which actually means "lost money or made less than $500"), "95% rugged within a day", "0.4% of wallets made more than $10k", LetsBonk's 411 SOL threshold, and the 12M-instruction Groth16 figure. The Solidus Labs 98.6% "rug or pump-and-dump" label is a vendor classification, not a measured loss rate ([BeInCrypto](https://beincrypto.com/pump-fun-tokens-scams-solidus-labs-report/)). Bain's distribution yield appears as both 11% and 14%, and both are unverified.

### Why the curve shape matters

pump.fun's curve is P(x) = (30 + x)² / (30 · 1.073×10⁹), so the price rises **(115/30)² ≈ 14.7×** between launch and graduation at 85 real SOL ([arXiv 2602.14860](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.14860v1)). For a dividend token, the implied yield then falls 14.7× along the curve. A token that graduates at an 18% yield started at roughly 260%, which hands a fundamentals-blind windfall to snipers. More generally, a constant-product curve with virtual quote reserve V that raises R ends at a price ratio of (1 + R/V)². Getting a 3× ratio needs R/V ≈ 0.73, against pump.fun's 2.83. Hooks graduates at about 2.93× its starting value ([Hooks](https://github.com/adrianvrj/hooks)). The simulation's central experiment should sweep this ratio. These calculations are inference from the cited curve parameters.

The agent classes should be the same in both arms, so the only difference is the asset. **Snipers** buy in the first blocks and exit at graduation. **Momentum traders** follow price and volume with a high turnover hazard. **Yield investors** buy when implied yield exceeds a hurdle (8% plus the hazard premium), lock, and hold. **Arbitrageurs** close gaps between the curve, the AMM and the SDEX. **LPs** provide liquidity. **Companies** are either honest, delayed payers, misreporters or failures. Measure, for each class and model: the return distribution (median, mean, P(loss), 1st/99th percentile), annualized IRR, and Sharpe/Sortino. At market level, measure the Gini coefficient and Lorenz curve of wallet P&L; a sum-of-P&L check (it must equal minus fees and value extracted); turnover and volume per dollar of fundamental value; the share of supply locked; price ÷ fundamental value over time; realized yield to lockers (dividends ÷ the locked share's market value, which rises when few holders lock); graduation rate and time to graduation; and maximum drawdown.

Six charts would carry the argument. First, overlaid log-scale P&L CDFs for memecoin, dividend-curve, real-estate token and BDC. Second, a fan chart of price versus fundamental value through curve, graduation and four dividend epochs. Third, a heatmap of yield-investor IRR across curve ratio and payout X%. Fourth, sniper share of total profit against curve ratio. Fifth, Lorenz curves per model. Sixth, survival-style curves of tokens still paying dividends against memecoins still trading. The hypothesis to test is that dividends plus full-epoch locks cut turnover and shift profit from snipers to yield investors. Flattening the curve should be the single largest lever, and the fraud rate the largest risk. If the simulation shows that the 14.7× pump.fun ratio still lets snipers capture most of the gains even with dividends, that finding itself argues for the flatter curve.

## Conclusion

The notes point to a reframing. What makes this design suit Stellar is less the ZK and more that Stellar's 2015-era asset authorization flags let a ZK proof become a protocol-level permission. Every native venue then inherits compliance for free, which hook-based tokens on other chains can't do without allowlisting each pool. The real open risks sit outside the code. Nothing on-chain forces a company to wire profits, so the agreement, KYB depth and fraud-rate assumptions decide investor outcomes more than any contract does. A Reg S distribution for equity of a US issuer carries resale limits that the notes didn't quantify. And a gated market structurally loses the impulse buyers who drive pump.fun volume, which may well be the point.

For the hackathon, the winning narrative is measurable. Put four contract IDs and six transaction hashes (proof verified, trustline authorized, curve buy, native-pool graduation, `distribute`, claim) in the architecture doc. Add one simulation chart showing that a flattened curve with epoch locks moves returns from snipers to yield investors. Against Stellar's roughly $4B RWA base, which today holds no private equity, that would make this the first credible on-chain answer to the private-equity distribution drought: distribution yields of about **6% against a 16% pre-2020 norm** ([Dakota/McKinsey summary](https://news.dakota.com/p/mckinsey-cambridge-bcg-review-2025-pe-data)).

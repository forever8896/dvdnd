# Private Equity Dividend Data: Market Shift, SME Economics, Dividend Analogs, Simulation Parameters

Research date: 2026-10-25. Most figures are from 2025–2026. Each one is dated and its geography is given. Where only a secondary or aggregator source was found, it is labeled as such.

## 1. Market shift: is sophisticated capital moving from public to private markets?

### Takeaway
The long-run trend toward private markets holds up. The US listed-company count has roughly halved since its 1996 peak. Preqin forecasts about $32T in alternatives AUM by 2030, and US policy (the August 2025 401(k) executive order and the DOL proposal) is opening the retail and defined-contribution channel. The near-term picture is different. In 2025, closed-end PE fundraising was down and distribution yields were at multi-year lows, so the "shift" is now carried by private wealth and evergreen structures more than by institutions. Tokenized private credit is real but still small: single-digit billions of distributed value on rwa.xyz.

### Cited Findings
- **Preqin, "Private Markets in 2030" (released Oct 2025, global):** forecasts global alternatives AUM of about **$32 trillion by 2030**. Private credit (direct lending and other strategies) is forecast to reach **about $4.5T by 2030** and infrastructure about $3T. Preqin expects private wealth to take a growing share of private-market fundraising while institutional fundraising stays constrained, and says new, more liquid fund structures will support private credit fundraising. — [BusinessWire / Preqin press release](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20251016075672/en/Preqin-Releases-Private-Markets-in-2030-Report); [HedgeCo summary](https://www.hedgeco.net/news/?p=90838)
- **McKinsey Global Private Markets Report 2026 (Feb 2026, global, via secondary summary):** closed-end PE fundraising fell **17% YoY to about $616B** in 2025. Distributions were **about 6% (of AUM/NAV)**, against a **2015–2019 average of 16%**. The summary's wording ("distributions to paid-in capital … 6% of AUM") is ambiguous, so treat this as a distribution yield, not DPI. — [Dakota News summary](https://news.dakota.com/p/mckinsey-cambridge-bcg-review-2025-pe-data). I could not reach the primary report (the McKinsey page timed out).
- **McKinsey 2026, more (secondary):** deal value **rose 19% in 2025** and IPOs showed signs of life, but the report frames the rebound as exposing structural problems. — [Alternatives Watch](https://www.alternativeswatch.com/2026/02/19/private-equitys-comeback-year-masks-deeper-structural-shifts-mckinsey-report-finds/). GP-led continuation vehicles grew from **$35B (2020) to $115B (2025)**. — [Not Very Private Equity, 2/19/26](https://www.notveryprivateequity.com/news-2-19-26/)
- **Cambridge Associates (secondary):** fundraising volumes **troughed in 2025 at about one-third of 2021 levels**, and CA expects the distribution slowdown to continue into 2026. — [Dakota News summary](https://news.dakota.com/p/mckinsey-cambridge-bcg-review-2025-pe-data)
- **Bain Global PE Report 2026 (global):** Bain describes the 2025 rebound as narrow, with LP distributions still lagging exit values. Bain's own DPI figures were not retrievable. Secondary blogs put distributions at **11% of NAV (2024, a record low)** or **about 14% in 2025, after four years below 15%**. These two figures conflict and are unverified. — [Bain report page](https://www.bain.com/insights/global-private-equity-report-2019/); [Libertify summary](https://www.libertify.com/interactive-library/bain-global-private-equity-report-2026/); [Angel Investors Network](https://angelinvestorsnetwork.com/private-equity/pe-recovery-2026-distribution-acceleration-and-momentum-building-in-ma-dealmakin)
- **Unverified (blog citing Bain 2026):** about **$3.2T of unrealized value across about 29,000 unsold companies**, and an average buyout hold period near 7 years. — [Angel Investors Network](https://angelinvestorsnetwork.com/market-analysis/dpi-only-metric-that-matters-private-equity-lps)
- **Decline of US public companies (US):** listed companies **peaked in 1996**. One CRSP-based count fell from **more than 8,000 (1996) to about 3,700**. Doidge, Karolyi and Stulz attribute about **55% of the net shortfall to fewer new listings and about 45% to more delistings** (mostly acquisitions). Smaller firms became less likely to list after 1996. — [Equitable Growth](https://equitablegrowth.org/explains-decline-u-s-public-corporations/); [DTNext/CNN](https://www.dtnext.in/news/business/number-of-publicly-held-companies-shrinking-in-us-717753); [Conversable Economist](https://conversableeconomist.com/2017/03/02/the-decline-in-us-public-companies/). A 2017 EY analysis disagrees in part: the domestic listed count has **stabilized since about 2008**, and the remaining firms are larger. — [EY via SEC](https://www.sec.gov/spotlight/investor-advisory-committee-2012/ey-an-analysis-of-trends-in-the-us-capital-markets.pdf)
- **401(k) access (US):** on **Aug 7, 2025** President Trump signed the executive order "Democratizing Access to Alternative Assets for 401(k) Investors" (covering private equity, real estate and digital assets). It directs DOL to clarify fiduciary duties and possible safe harbors, and the SEC to revisit accredited investor and qualified purchaser definitions. — [Mayer Brown](https://www.mayerbrown.com/ja/insights/publications/2025/08/president-trump-signs-executive-order-seeking-to-expand-availability-of-alternative-assets-in-401k-plans); [Mondaq ("$12.2T opportunity")](https://www.mondaq.com/unitedstates/financial-services/1664492/us%24122-trillion-opportunityexecutive-order-paves-the-way-for-easier-401k-plan-access-to-alternative-investments)
- **DOL proposal (US):** it lists six factors fiduciaries must weigh: performance, fees, liquidity, valuation, benchmarks and complexity. It is neutral on asset class. Critics cite fees, transparency and liquidity. Its finalization status was not confirmed. — [Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/employee-benefits/trump-floats-401k-rule-easing-way-for-private-equity-in-plans)
- **Tokenized RWAs (rwa.xyz via secondary sources, global, on-chain):**
  - Tokenized credit was **$5.01B "distributed" and $21.21B "represented" as of May 1, 2026**, and **about $6.93B distributed (July 2026)** within **$34.67B total distributed RWA value**. — [KuCoin news flash](https://www.kucoin.com/news/flash/rwa-tokenized-assets-drop-to-34-67b-figure-heloc-token-leads-with-20-1b); [MEXC news](https://www.mexc.com/news/528329)
  - Total distributed RWA was **about $38.82B on Sep 15, 2026**. — [Eco (low reliability)](https://eco.com/support/en/articles/15254020)
  - BlockEden claims private credit is **about 54% of on-chain RWA**, with an active book of **more than $18.9B**. This uses a different, broader measure. — [BlockEden, May 2026](https://blockeden.xyz/blog/2026/05/04/tokenized-private-credit-54-percent-rwa-apollo-centrifuge-maple/)

### Inferences
- The thesis should say "access is moving down-market," not "institutions are piling in." Institutional PE fundraising fell in 2025, while the policy and product push (401(k) order, evergreen and wealth vehicles, tokenization) aims to bring retail and wealth capital into private assets.
- The 2023–2026 "distribution drought" supports a cash-yield product. LPs are starved of DPI, and distribution yields are at about half their pre-2020 norm. A product built around recurring cash distributions answers the very pain point that classic closed-end PE creates.
- Tokenized private equity as a category is negligible next to tokenized credit and Treasuries. Tokenized credit (about $5–7B distributed, mid-2026) is the closest live comparable, and it shows investors want yield-bearing on-chain RWAs more than equity upside.

### Gaps

- No primary figure for evergreen/interval fund AUM or for private-wealth allocations to alternatives turned up.
- The current US listed-company count (2025–2026) was not verified from a primary source (WFE/CRSP).
- I could not read rwa.xyz directly. All tokenization figures are secondary and use mixed definitions (distributed vs represented).

## 2. Private equity returns vs public equity, and the distribution drought

### Takeaway
US PE returned 8.7% in 2025 (CA index) and has trailed public markets over most horizons under 10 years. Outperformance shows up more consistently over 10+ years, and more often against the Russell 2000 than the S&P 500. Distributions recovered in nominal terms in 2025 (US: about $185B distributed vs about $145B called), but the distribution yield was below its historical average for the fourth year running.

### Cited Findings
- **Cambridge Associates US PE/VC, calendar year 2025 (US):**
  - Index returns in 2025: **US PE 8.7%**, **US VC 21.1%**, growth equity 11.9%, buyouts 7.6%.
  - US PE **trailed public markets over most periods shorter than ten years**, and beat the Russell 2000 more often than the S&P 500.
  - Distributions were **about $185B (up about 5%)** and contributions **about $145B (down nearly 10%)**. Distributions exceeded contributions in both 2024 and 2025, but the **distribution yield (distributions/NAV) was below its historical average for the fourth consecutive year**.
  - Exits: **27 PE-backed IPOs (about $126B combined valuation)**; PE-backed M&A plus sponsor-to-sponsor deals of about $550B, about 1.6x 2024.
  - [CA US PE/VC Benchmark Commentary CY2025](https://publishedresearch.cambridgeassociates.com/us-pe-vc-benchmark-commentary-calendar-year-2025/)
- **CA, mid-2025 note:** "in periods ten years and longer, PE's outperformance is more consistent." — [Cambridge Associates](https://www.cambridgeassociates.com/insight/us-pe-vc-benchmark-commentary-calendar-year-2025/)
- **CA H1 2025, via secondary source:** US PE managers distributed **$78.9B vs $67.6B called**. — [Angel Investors Network](https://angelinvestorsnetwork.com/market-analysis/dpi-only-metric-that-matters-private-equity-lps)
- **Distribution yield:** McKinsey 2026 puts it at about **6% vs a 16% average for 2015–2019**. — [Dakota News](https://news.dakota.com/p/mckinsey-cambridge-bcg-review-2025-pe-data)

### Inferences
- For a simulation, a reasonable benchmark for diversified PE net returns is **about 8–13% p.a. long-run**. The 8.7% in 2025 is sourced; the upper range is an inference from CA's "outperformance over 10+ years" framing, so long-horizon numbers still need verifying. The cash yield on PE NAV ran **about 6–14% per year in 2024–2025**, against a pre-2020 norm of **about 16%**.
- The time-weighted gap between IRR and DPI is the key weakness of PE that a dividend-token product would compete against.

### Gaps
- The 10/15/20/25-year CA horizon returns and mPME spreads appear only in an image table in the CA commentary and were not extractable.
- No Burgiss/MSCI figures were retrieved.

## 3. Small and mid-sized private business economics

### Takeaway
Small US businesses sell for about **2.6–2.7x seller's discretionary earnings (SDE)**, a gross "earnings yield" of about 37% before paying for a replacement owner-operator. Survival data (BLS) shows about **22% of new establishments gone after 1 year, about 49% after 5 years and about 65% after 10 years**. Mature businesses fail at about **7%/yr** after year 5. Net margins vary widely by sector, from about 1% in grocery and healthcare support to about 25% in software. The cross-sector public-company median is about 8.6–9.7%.

### Inferences
- **Annualized hazard rates from the BLS data (my calculations):**
  - Year 1: about 21–22%.
  - Years 2–5: (51.5/79.2)^(1/4) gives about **10%/yr**.
  - Years 6–10: (34.9/50.8)^(1/5) gives about **7%/yr**.
  - A platform that lists only profitable businesses with 3+ years of operating history should model about **6–10%/yr total-loss risk** before adding fraud risk. These are establishment-level figures; closures also include voluntary exits that are not losses.
- **Earnings yield:** a 2.6–2.7x SDE multiple implies about **37–38% SDE yield** on purchase price. SDE includes owner pay, though. After paying a manager (for example $60–100k on a $156k SDE, which is an assumption), distributable profit is about $55–95k, a **cash yield of about 16–27%** on a $350k price. This is in line with the 15–50% earnings-yield framing in the brief, at its lower-middle.
- **Profit margin distribution for the sim:** use SDE margin about 15–25% (BizBuySell median about 22.5%) when owners operate. For manager-run net margin, use **about 5–12%**, centered near 8.5% (Damodaran ex-financials), with sector dispersion from 1% to 25%. A lognormal or beta fit centered near 8% with a long right tail is reasonable.

### Gaps

- No data on owner distribution and payout practices (share of profit distributed vs reinvested) for private SMEs.
- No 2025–2026 lower-middle-market EBITDA multiples (for example GF Data) were retrieved.
- BLS survival is establishment-level and covers all new firms, not profitable ones specifically. No survival data conditioned on profitability was found.

## 4. Dividend-paying analogs and comparable structures

### Takeaway
Comparable payout structures cluster at **about 6–8% cash yield** for listed vehicles that lend to or invest in lower-middle-market firms (MAIN, BDCs). Revenue-based financing caps (1.3–2x) work out to **about 25–40% IRR** under fast growth. Search funds return about 34% pooled IRR, driven by outliers. Reg CF shows about 2.5x / 22% IRR on exits, but outcomes are highly skewed and illiquid. Tokenized real estate (RealT) shows nominal rental yields of about 3–14%, while secondary token prices are often far below issue.

### Cited Findings
- **Main Street Capital (MAIN, US-listed BDC that invests in lower-middle-market private companies):**
  - Yield differs by source and date: forward yield **5.95% (Jul 25, 2026)** per [Digrin](https://www.digrin.com/stocks/detail/MAIN/), **7.77% (Jul 28, 2026)** per [Dividend.watch](https://dividend.watch/symbol/MAIN-NYSE), and **7.48% on a $4.38 annual dividend (Aug 19, 2026)** per [StockAnalysis](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/main/dividend/). The spread comes from how special dividends are treated.
  - Pays monthly (about $0.265 in mid-2026) plus recurring specials of about $0.30.
  - NAV per share about **$33.46 (record)**, with the stock at **about 1.5x NAV**. — [Walnut Invest](https://walnutinvest.com/stocks/main/dividend)
  - Annual total returns 2020–2025 of 34.8%, −19.4%, 47.1%, −11.5%, 24.9% and 46.7% (year labels unverified). Forward total-return estimate **about 5.3%/yr**, mostly dividend. — [Sure Dividend Aug 2026](https://www.suredividend.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/MAIN-2026-08-09.pdf)
- **Search funds (US/Canada):**
  - **Stanford 2026 study (data to Dec 31, 2025):** aggregate **IRR 33.9%, ROI 4.75x** across about 862 search funds (1984–2025). — [Stanford GSB, Jul 2026](https://www.gsb.stanford.edu/index.php/insights/search-funds-keep-offering-proven-path-ownership)
  - **Stanford 2024 study:** **IRR 35.1%, ROI 4.5x** (681 funds); exited-company IRR 42.9%.
  - Excluding 10x+ outliers gives **about 2.8x / 27% IRR**, and **about 26% of acquired companies lose capital** (secondary summaries).
  - [Searchfunder](https://searchfunder.com/post/stanford-2024-search-fund-study-highlights); [Altss](https://altss.com/glossary/search-fund)
- **Reg CF / Reg A+ (US):**
  - **KingsCrowd 2018–2024:** of 6,375 companies, 77 exits (1.2%) and 186 failures (2.9%). These are discoverable outcomes only, so true failure is understated. — [KingsCrowd](https://kingscrowd.com/article/investment-crowdfunding-exits-vs-failures-a-2018-2024-data-analysis/)
  - **KingsCrowd mid-2026:** 3-year failure rate of **10.9% for the 2018 cohort vs 3.7% for 2022**. Mean multiple on exits **about 2.5x over 4.5 years (about 22% IRR)**, and **10% of exits produced 85% of value**. — [KingsCrowd mid-2026](https://kingscrowd.com/mid-year-2026-investment-crowdfunding-exits-failures/)
  - 2025 YTD: 19 exits vs 35 failures. — [Crowdfund Insider](https://www.crowdfundinsider.com/?p=254683)
  - An SEC-commissioned study associates a successful Reg CF raise with about **9% lower startup failure** and **2% higher odds of follow-on funding**. — [Forvis Mazars](https://forvismazars.us/getmedia/1c3d9a95-3447-4643-874a-585601cd6539/SEC-to-Consider-Crowdfunding-Changes-V2.pdf)
  - Angel benchmark (Wiltbank): **52–56% of angel investments returned less than 1x**, and about 7–9% returned more than 10x. — [CrowdWise](https://crowdwise.org/top/equity-crowdfunding-returns/)
- **Revenue-based financing:**
  - Caps are **typically 1.3x–2.0x** of capital, owed in full regardless of duration. IRR depends on repayment speed. — [Dealplexus](https://www.dealplexus.com/blog/how-revenue-based-financing-works-in-india-and-when-it-beats-equity-dilution); [Capchase](https://capchase.com/blog/revenue-based-financing)
  - Vendor example: a 1.6x cap gives about **38% effective annual cost at 8% monthly growth**, falling to **about 25% at 3% monthly growth** (34-month payback). — [Dealplexus](https://www.dealplexus.com/blog/how-revenue-based-financing-works-in-india-and-when-it-beats-equity-dilution)
- **Tokenized real estate (RealT, US properties, on-chain):**
  - An unofficial tracker shows per-property "current yield" mostly about **3–14%** (for example 13.92% on one Detroit property). The same tracker shows secondary-market (YAM) token values **often 50–85% below the RealT issue price**. Undated, third-party data. — [RealT rent tracker (unofficial)](https://ehpst.duckdns.org/realt_rent_tracker/portfolio/17655)
  - No official current RealT yield summary was found. The commonly cited ~8–11% gross figure is unverified here.

### Inferences
- Revenue-share or dividend tokens priced near cash yields of **6–10%** sit alongside MAIN and BDCs, and those vehicles are diversified, audited and listed. A single-SME token must offer a substantial premium (for example **15–25% target cash yield**) to make up for idiosyncratic failure risk (about 7–10%/yr), illiquidity and fraud.
- The RealT case is a cautionary data point. A high nominal yield on issue price can coexist with large secondary-price drawdowns. For the bonding-curve design, the price should reflect realized distributions and risk, and the curve could inflate prices beyond what the cash flows support.
- Search-fund and Reg CF returns are outlier-driven equity returns. A dividend platform's return profile looks more like RBF or private credit: capped upside, steady yield, loss-driven downside.

### Gaps
- No BDC sector-average yield (for example the Cliffwater BDC Index or the VanEck BDC ETF) and no REIT average yield (Nareit) were retrieved.
- No since-IPO MAIN total return figure was found in primary IR materials.
- No independent portfolio-level RBF loss rates or realized investor IRRs (for example Lighter Capital or Clearco) were found.
- No RealT official yield or default/vacancy statistics were found.

## 5. Suggested simulation parameters (synthesis)

### Takeaway
Below is a parameter set anchored to the sourced data above. Rows marked (inferred) are my judgment built from the cited anchors, not direct source figures.

### Cited Findings (anchors)
- Net margin by sector: 1–25%; ex-financial median **8.56%** (Jan 2026, US public). — [Damodaran](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~adamodar/New_Home_Page/datafile/margin.html)
- SDE margin about 22.5% (my calculation from medians); purchase multiple **2.6–2.7x SDE** (2025–Q2 2026, US). — [BizBuySell](https://www.bizbuysell.com/news/media_insight.html)
- Survival: 77.9% (1-yr), 51.5% (5-yr), 34.9% (10-yr) (BLS, US, to March 2025). — [BLS](https://www.bls.gov/bdm/us_age_naics_00_table7.txt)
- Yield comps: MAIN about 6–7.8% (mid-2026). — [StockAnalysis](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/main/dividend/). PE distribution yield about 6% vs 16% historical. — [Dakota/McKinsey](https://news.dakota.com/p/mckinsey-cambridge-bcg-review-2025-pe-data)
- Equity outlier comps: search funds 33.9% IRR / 4.75x. — [Stanford GSB](https://www.gsb.stanford.edu/index.php/insights/search-funds-keep-offering-proven-path-ownership). Reg CF exits 2.5x / 22% IRR. — [KingsCrowd](https://kingscrowd.com/mid-year-2026-investment-crowdfunding-exits-failures/)

### Inferences
Proposed parameters for each simulated company and its token (all inferred):

| Parameter | Suggested distribution | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue (annual) | Lognormal, median $700k, sigma about 0.8 | BizBuySell median revenue $692k |
| Net profit margin (manager-run) | Beta or truncated normal, mean 9%, sd 6%, range −5% to 30% | Damodaran ex-financials 8.56%; sector spread 1–25% |
| Profit growth (annual, mean) | Normal, mean 3–5%, sd 10–15% | Unsourced gap: roughly nominal GDP plus noise; small firms are volatile |
| Profit volatility (year-to-year) | 20–35% annual sd of profit | Inferred: small-firm cash flows are lumpy; Trucking/Restaurants at the high end |
| Annual failure (total-loss) hazard | 10%/yr in years 1–5 of listing if young; 7%/yr if 5+ years old; choose 6–8% for vetted profitable firms | BLS survival tables |
| Fraud / misreporting probability | 1–3%/yr (scenario parameter) | No source found; set as a stress-test variable |
| Recovery on failure | 0–20% of token value | Inferred: equity-like junior claim |
| Payout ratio (share of net profit distributed) | 50–90%; base case 70% | BDC/REIT pass-through norms are about 90% (well known, not retrieved this session); SMEs need retained capex |
| Fundamental value at listing | 3–5x net profit (manager-run) | Inferred: 2.6–2.7x SDE adjusted for replacement-owner cost |
| Implied dividend yield on fundamental value | 14–25% (base about 18%) | payout ratio ÷ (price/earnings); for example 0.7 / 4x = 17.5% |
| Bonding-curve implied yield | yield_t = (payout × trailing profit) ÷ (curve price × supply) | Track the gap between curve price and fundamental value; compare with RealT secondary discounts of 50–85% |
| Benchmark hurdle for "attractive" | more than 8% (MAIN/BDC) after expected losses | Net yield ≈ gross yield − annual hazard × (1 − recovery), for example 18% − 7% × 0.9 ≈ 11.7% |

- Expected net cash return for a diversified token basket at an 18% gross yield, a 7% hazard and 10% recovery is **about 11–12%/yr** before growth and fees. That is above MAIN and BDCs (about 6–8%) and roughly in line with PE long-run returns, with more frequent cash distributions. Concentrated single-name positions still face about a 50% chance of failure within 5–10 years (BLS).

### Gaps
- Profit growth and volatility for private SMEs are not sourced. They should be calibrated against Sageworks/BizMiner or Fed SBCS data in a later pass.
- No base-rate data on fraud and misreporting in SME crowdfunding was found.
- The BDC 90% distribution rule and REIT yields come from general knowledge and were not verified this session.
